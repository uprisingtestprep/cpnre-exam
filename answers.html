Case CPNRE-C010

On a long-term care unit with twelve residents, six residents developed watery diarrhea over two days. Samuel Greco, 79, was the first and also has a draining sacral wound. Staff share one commode between rooms, and some visitors skip hand hygiene. The practical nurse is helping the infection control team with the response and reports to the unit RN daily.

  1. The correct answer is C (Mode of transmission by indirect contact, since a contaminated object carries organisms between residents.): A contaminated shared object carrying organisms from one resident to another is indirect contact, which is a mode of transmission. Portal of exit is the body site where organisms leave the person. The susceptible host is the person at risk. Portal of entry is where organisms get into the body, such as the mouth.
  2. The correct answer is A (Remove gloves and wash hands with soap and water before entering the next room.): Contaminated hands and gloves carry organisms between rooms, and with spores suspected, soap and water removes them better than alcohol rub. Alcohol rub is less effective against spores. Reused gloves spread organisms between residents. Antibiotics do not break transmission and are not given to residents without infection.
  3. The correct answer is A (Standard practices cover every resident, so gloves are worn when soiling is likely.): Standard practices treat all clients as potentially infectious, so soiling risk, not symptoms, triggers glove use. Waiting for a confirmed infection misses people who carry organisms without signs. Hand hygiene alone does not protect the nurse's hands when soiling is expected. The roommate is exposed to the same environment, so a matching diagnosis is not required.
  4. The correct answer is D (Judging the task, the resident's condition, and the setting to pick protective equipment.): A point-of-care risk assessment is a quick, situation-specific judgement of the task, the resident, and the setting, so Samuel's draining wound and diarrhea, along with the splash risk, shape the equipment. A door sign sets a minimum, not every task's needs. Waiting for lab results leaves the nurse unprotected meanwhile. Comfort and stock are not risk factors.
  5. The correct answer is D (Both values fall within the expected ranges for a healthy sleeping infant of this age.): Expected infant values are much higher than adult values, and 36 breaths with a heart rate of 120 are normal for an 8-month-old, even asleep. Adult limits do not apply to infants. A rate of 120 does not call for urgent reporting. Without other signs such as retractions or color change, there is no sign of distress.
  6. The correct answer is C (Auscultate the apical pulse for a full minute.): The apical pulse is the most reliable site in infants because the radial pulse is hard to feel, and counting for a full minute accounts for variation. Repeating the radial attempt wastes time. A 15 second count multiplies errors in a crying infant. The carotid is hard to palpate in a short infant neck and pressure there is unsafe.

Employer policy cannot widen what legislation allows.
Case CPNRE-C006
